    Digital humans: Character Creator in Studio and a MetaHuman adapter

    A character appearance editor right in Studio — face blending, sculpting, skin, makeup, hair, body and outfit try-on — and an adapter that recognises MetaHuman exports with no Unreal at runtime.

    Character Creator has its own Character workspace. The face blends on an archetype circle, and single parts — nose, mouth, eyes, jaw — get their own handle and are picked with a click in the viewport. Sculpting works by dragging with a brush circle, skin, eye, hair and body sliders update the model live, and one shared history undoes with ⌘Z.

    Makeup (lipstick, blush, eyeshadow, stubble) is layered on the skin texture and finds the lips, cheeks and eyelids from the face geometry. Clothing and hairstyles are tried on in the viewport without a build; the build adds LODs for skinned parts, shows a triangle budget and places the character in the game as a unit type in one click.

    The MetaHuman adapter reads an ordinary glTF / GLB / FBX export: it recognises the UE5 skeleton and face meshes, assigns material roles, selects the face meshes and counts how many of the 52 ARKit facial channels the model has. *_half corrective joints and forearm twists, as well as the jaw and eyes, follow those channels when the morphs do not already move them. Draco-compressed GLB is decoded by an offline script.

    Verified
    Studio Mac build, CTest 9/9 PASS including vivid_metahuman_adapter on three real MetaHuman exports (Ada, Bruce, Draco-decoded Bruce) and vivid_human_lod; web tests 149/149. Screenshots are native Studio output without retouching.
    Limitations
    Not full RigLogic: bulge, in/out, fwd/bck correctives stay in bind pose; groom is cards only; .dna is read only by the offline tool; the Ada export has no tongueOut channel. Makeup placement uses the nearest skin vertices; unusual UVs may need a manual tweak.
  2. Nov 21, 2026
    Donemain · 864bd2d8c

    Digital humans: soft facial shadows with adaptive resolution

    Shadow map resolution follows the character's size on screen, and the studio light gives a soft portrait shadow — no steps on the neck or ragged edge by the nose.

    A shared CPU AdaptiveShadowResolution policy picks a 1024, 2048 or 4096 shadow map from viewport height, camera distance and field of view and the share of the frame the character covers. It upgrades at 15% excess demand and downgrades only after 60 frames of low demand, so the resolution does not jump.

    The VIVID portrait key light uses soft PCF instead of the general PCSS: an artistic facial filter, not a physically exact area light. Gameplay lighting and shaders are unchanged.

    Verified
    CTest adaptive_shadow_resolution: 1/1 PASS in Release with assertions; native VIVID Creator and Studio builds; video of 168 frames at 1500×920, 24 fps, 0 pipeline failures. Frames are native output without retouching.
    Limitations
    The density estimate is conservative, not per pixel; these are not cascaded or virtual shadow maps. Lower resolution tiers at full body are not confirmed with this asset.
