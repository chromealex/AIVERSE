Every stage of PRISMA with screenshots, what was verified and what was not yet.

  1. Nov 22, 2026
    Donemain · d677f8a2f

    Depth of field and motion blur are regular effects

    Depth of field and motion blur are no longer experimental: add the effect to a camera profile and it works on Metal, OpenGL and Vulkan.

    Both effects used to need an environment variable. Now `depthOfField` and `motionBlur` in a project's `postfx` or a camera profile work right away, like bloom or colour grading. They default to the High quality tier, motion blur needs an HDR frame. `AIVERSE_PRISMA_DOF=0` and `AIVERSE_PRISMA_MOTION_BLUR=0` remain to turn them off.

    Depth of field works from hardware depth or from distance in metres along the camera (`metricFocus`); LENS sets the focus from lens parameters. Motion blur follows camera motion and the velocity of moving objects.

    Verified
    The same HDR scene on Metal and OpenGL (Mac) and on Vulkan (Android, Adreno 610): focused on the scene the frame stays sharp, focused near the camera it blurs clearly; a turning camera with motion blur smears the frame, a still one is left alone. PRISMA's report marks each effect as drawn.
    Limitations
    An approximation, not a physical lens model: no hexagonal bokeh or depth layer separation. On weak mobile GPUs both stay off by default below the High tier.

  2. Nov 22, 2026
    Donefeature/aiv20261122_prisma_effects · b7bf96e9

    AO, reflections, lens effects, SMAA and FSR

    PRISMA gains ambient occlusion, screen-space reflections, lens flare and dirt, distortion, anamorphic streaks, halation, heat haze with shockwaves, SMAA, FSR 1 upscaling and histogram exposure.

    The new effects go into a project's, map's or camera profile's `postfx`, like bloom: `ambientOcclusion`, `screenSpaceReflections`, `lensFlare` (with a dirt texture or procedural smudges), `lensDistortion` (barrel, pincushion, Panini), `anamorphicStreaks`, `halation`, `heatHaze`, `smaa`, `fsr`. Auto exposure can meter from a luminance histogram (`histogram`, low and high percentiles), so a bright sky or a dark corner no longer throws exposure off.

    AO and reflections work from the depth buffer: normals are reconstructed from depth, reflections are found by marching a ray through the scene. FSR 1 (EASU + RCAS) renders the 3D scene at a lower resolution and scales it to the screen; a DLSS route exists, but its NGX adapter has not been built yet. A `sceneColor` node in Shader Graph lets post effects read the frame; the stylization presets use it: toon, sketch, CRT, scanlines, dithering, Kuwahara.

    Every effect has quality tiers and a cost estimate, and PRISMA's report shows whether it was drawn.

    Verified
    Mac Studio (M3 Ultra), commit b7bf96e9: 18 tests on Metal (each of the 12 effects is drawn and changes the frame the expected way, plus the PRISMA data and Shader Graph tests) and 12 on OpenGL — all passed. On Linux the same 12 scenes passed on Vulkan (software lavapipe). The first Mac run found banding in AO, broken-up reflections, an upside-down FSR frame on Metal and smeared streaks — fixed before this run.
    Limitations
    DLSS was not built or tested (needs Windows, RTX and the NGX SDK). Vulkan was checked only on a software renderer, not on real GPUs or Android. AO and SSR work from depth without a G-buffer: surfaces reflect by normal slope, not by material; thin objects may be noisy. GPU cost was not measured.

  3. Nov 21, 2026
    In progressagent-wind-masks · 6f7652aae

    PRISMA: a camera at the water surface — waterline, film and drops

    At the surface the frame splits along a waving waterline with a meniscus; after emerging, everything that was under water is covered by a refracting film that drains to the water level, leaving rivulets and drops.

    lensWater tests the lens point of every pixel against NEREID's waving water plane: underwater optics below the line, the dry image above it, a bright refracting meniscus along it. Nothing runs down the part of the lens that is under water.

    After emerging the film clings for the first third of sheetTime, then its ragged edge drains down, leaving meandering rivulets with a head drop and round lens drops with glints that dry over dryTime. Every parameter is tunable and the effect can be disabled.

    Verified
    Mac (Metal), AlpineValley review copy: a real-time dive recording (a frame every ~0.35 s, video assembled at real speed); prisma_lens_water test.
    Limitations
    The underside of the surface (total internal reflection) is not rendered separately yet; other backends are not visually verified.

  4. Nov 21, 2026
    Donemain · 6f7e1861c

    Depth of field: the blurred foreground is no longer cut out

    Blurred foreground objects now spread over the background as in a real lens instead of ending on a hard line, as if cut out with scissors. The background still does not bleed onto a sharp foreground.

    Before, blur at a depth boundary threw away everything on the other side: a near curtain or ivy kept a crisp outline over a blurred background, with ghosted patches next to it. Now every point of the frame gathers light from neighbours whose blur disc reaches it. Near blurred objects cover the background and the sharp zone, and what is behind them shows through their edge.

    The rule respects sharpness: the background cannot bleed onto an object sharper than itself, so a sharp object in focus keeps a clean edge. The change is in PRISMA's metric depth of field, used by the LENS lens and the metricFocus setting.

    Verified
    A before/after pair was rendered on a Mac (Metal) in Sponza with the same frame: a 35 mm f/1.4 lens focused at 4 m.
    Limitations
    The new shader has not been compiled on OpenGL, WebGL or Vulkan yet. The pass is more expensive (32 samples per pixel instead of 8); its cost on weak mobile GPUs has not been measured.
