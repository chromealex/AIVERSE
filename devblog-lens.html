Development diary: LENS

Every stage of LENS with screenshots, what was verified and what was not yet.

  1. Nov 22, 2026
    Donemain · 8daa28bad

    LENS: split-screen, director and visibility layers

    Two cameras share the screen and each has its own processing — for example a colour main view next to a black-and-white one. A camera can skip whole layers of the scene. The director blends smoothly from one camera to another when priorities change.

    Split-screen, overlays and picture-in-picture are assembled in one screen pass. PRISMA post-processing can now be set per camera: grading, grain, vignette, depth of field. Lens exposure and focus are kept.

    Scene objects have visibility layers, cameras have a layer mask; in a scene it is the layers key of a model instance. In the frame the left camera sees a sword on layer 2, the right camera with a mask without that layer does not draw it; without the mask the sword appears on the right too.

    The director picks the highest-priority camera and, on a switch, blends position, rotation and field of view from the old camera to the new one. The strip shows frames before the switch, in the middle of the blend and after it.

    Verified
    All frames rendered on a Mac (Metal) in Sponza at 1920×1080. The 1 s director blend was checked by its weights: 0.07 / 0.50 / 0.93 / 1.00 at frames 10 / 20 / 30 / 40.
    Limitations
    Layers filter models only; meshes, sprites, terrain and particles ignore the mask, and layers are not available in HLOD scenes. Studio does not edit layers or per-camera post-processing yet. Backends other than Metal are not checked in a frame.

  2. Nov 22, 2026
    Donemain · 8daa28bad

    LENS: panoramas, mirrors, portals and stereo

    A camera can capture six cube faces and assemble them into a 360° panorama, show the view of a mirror or a portal clipped at its plane, and render a pair of eyes for stereo — including from the eye poses and field-of-view angles an XR runtime provides.

    The panorama is built on the GPU from six 90° faces into an equirectangular projection, for reflections, previews and environment capture. A bug was found and fixed: the panorama used to come out upside down. The sun shadow in all six faces comes from one shared shadow map around the capture point, so lighting does not jump at the seams between faces.

    Mirror and portal cameras reflect or carry over the main camera's view and clip everything behind the mirror plane or the portal exit. In the frames they are shown as an inset over the main view.

    A stereo pair can come from one camera — by interpupillary distance and convergence distance — or from headset data: a pose per eye and four field-of-view tangents, asymmetric ones included.

    Verified
    All frames rendered on a Mac (Metal) in Sponza at 1920×1080: a 360° panorama from 512×512 faces, mirror and portal cameras as insets, stereo pairs from one camera and from XR-runtime-style data.
    Limitations
    OpenXR is not connected: headset output, swapchain, frame submission and reprojection stay with an XR host, and nothing has run on a headset. Mirrors and portals are shown as separate views, without a ready mirror surface in the scene. Backends other than Metal are not checked in a frame.

  3. Nov 22, 2026
    Donefeature/aiv20261122_camera_presets

    Camera presets: one line of data

    Cameras of every project are now built from the same bodies — map overview, follow, chase, first person and planet orbit — and any of them can be set with a ready preset in one line.

    The map camera, the camera following a hero, the camera behind a car and the first-person view used to live in different places, each configured its own way. Now they are four bodies of one LENS camera system, and a camera block starts from a preset: "camera": {"preset": "overhead-isometric"} gives an isometric view, "topDown": {"camera": {"preset": "follow-speed"}} a camera that pulls back and leads at speed. Any key on top of the preset overrides it.

    Eleven presets are ready: a perspective map view, a steep view, a pixel-perfect top view for sprite games, isometric, four follow variants (over the shoulder, straight down, with speed, telephoto from far away), two chase variants and first person. Presets are data; a new one needs no code.

    The ECS camera rig gained chase and first-person modes on the same math, and a follow rig with a follow block in cameras.json works like the game follow camera — with a preset, velocity lead and bounds. The Studio cameras window applies a preset in one pick, and agents get the list through the MCP tool lens_camera_presets. The planet camera became the system's fifth body — an orbit over a sphere. The preset and key list is on the documentation page “LENS: camera behaviours”.

    Verified
    macOS, Metal: frames captured in the game client on two templates (only the preset line in game.json changes) and on a planet map with the orbit camera. Camera, LENS MCP tool, top-down and FPS tests pass on Mac.
    Limitations
    The ECS follow body leads its target by velocity only: a scene has no stick. The chase camera and first-person look read rules and maps, not cameras.json.
  4. Nov 21, 2026
    Donemain · cd6633caf

    LENS: the lens sets exposure and depth of field

    A camera can be described as a real lens — focal length, aperture, shutter, ISO and focus distance — and the frame gets its exposure and depth of field from them. Smooth camera rails for fly-throughs, camera shake and HDR frame recording to EXR were added.

    Every camera in cameras.json now has a lens block. With driveExposure and driveDepthOfField on, the camera sets the frame brightness and the sharp zone itself: f/1.4 gives a short sharp zone and a strongly blurred background, f/8 keeps almost everything sharp. It is computed per camera, so the main view and the inset can use different lenses.

    For fly-throughs the camera follows a rail through several points at an even speed, eases in and out, and the rail can loop. Shake is noise on six axes with a fade, identical on every run. The old linear rails and the previous shake behave as before.

    Frames can be recorded as an OpenEXR sequence with the full HDR range, for editing, grading and render comparisons.

    Verified
    Rendered on a Mac (Metal) in Sponza at 1920×1080: a 35 mm f/1.4 lens focused at 4 m, with a second camera of the same scene in the inset. Exposure, depth of field, rails and EXR maths are covered by tests on Linux and Mac.
    Limitations
    HDR recording to EXR is Metal-only for now; OpenGL records an 8-bit frame. Exposure is relative to a default lens, not in physical light units. Cubemaps, panoramas, mirrors, portals and stereo are not done yet.
    docs/camera-system.md
  5. Nov 20, 2026
    Donemain

    LENS: cameras as scene data, a Studio window and agent tools

    A game's cameras are now described in one cameras.json file: several cameras, their draw order, their own render targets and frame capture. They can be created and edited in a Studio window or through the agent's MCP tools.

    Each camera is a scene entity with its own components: transform, lens, follow rig, render target. The engine orders the cameras itself when one shows the image of another and warns about cycles.

    Secondary cameras — monitors, picture-in-picture, portraits — get the same prepared frame as the main view: the game is not simulated again and animation does not step twice.

    Studio has a cameras window: list, create, save and reload. Agents do the same through MCP: lens_scene_get, lens_camera_set and the related operations validate a change before writing and leave the working file intact on error. Any camera's frame can be saved to PNG.

    Verified
    The cameras window was opened in Studio on a Mac with a project and its camera loaded; the client with cameras enabled rendered the game scene on Metal. Replaying the frame into a secondary camera was compared pixel by pixel with the main frame on Metal.
    Limitations
    The pixel comparison has only been run on Metal; Vulkan and mobile GPUs are not checked yet. Cost with many cameras has not been measured.
